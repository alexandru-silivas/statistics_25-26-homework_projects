Homework 11 — Simulating Brownian Motion (Wiener Process)

In this assignment we revisit the numerical simulation framework of Homework 10 (Euler–Maruyama method) and apply it to the simplest stochastic differential equation:

$$ dX_t = dW_t, \quad X_0 = 0 $$

This SDE defines standard Brownian Motion (or a Wiener process). Since the drift is zero and the diffusion coefficient is constant \(1\), the Euler–Maruyama update reduces to:

$$ X_{t+\Delta t} = X_t + \sqrt{\Delta t}\, Z, \quad Z \sim N(0,1). $$

This gives us a straightforward way to simulate Brownian motion numerically, provided we can generate normal random variables. Here we use the Box–Muller transform to generate \(N(0,1)\) samples from uniform random inputs.

1. From SDE to Numerics: Euler–Maruyama for Brownian Motion

Euler–Maruyama for a general SDE $$ dX_t = a(X_t,t) dt + b(X_t,t) dW_t $$ is:

$$ X_{t+\Delta t} = X_t + a(X_t,t)\Delta t + b(X_t,t)\sqrt{\Delta t}\, Z, $$

For Brownian motion we have \(a=0\) and \(b=1\), so the update is purely stochastic:

$$ X_{t+\Delta t} = X_t + \sqrt{\Delta t}\, Z. $$

The resulting approximations converge in distribution to the true Wiener process as \( n \to \infty \). This recreates key properties of Brownian motion:

  • Continuous paths (but nowhere differentiable)
  • Independent increments
  • Gaussian increments \( X_{t+\Delta t} - X_t \sim N(0, \Delta t) \)

2. Box–Muller Normal Generator

To simulate increments we need normally distributed random variables. Box–Muller is ideal for teaching purposes because it is explicit and exact:

$$ Z = \sqrt{-2 \ln U_1}\, \cos (2\pi U_2), \qquad U_1, U_2 \sim U(0,1). $$

Our Brownian increment is then:

$$ \Delta X = \sqrt{\Delta t}\, Z. $$

4. Interpretation and Theoretical Connections

Brownian motion is the continuous-time limit of discrete random walks, much like the Poisson process is the limit of Bernoulli counting processes. These are the two most fundamental building blocks of stochastic processes:

  • Poisson process — models counts of events
  • Brownian motion — models accumulated noise

Whereas the Poisson process jumps by integer amounts, Brownian motion evolves continuously with Gaussian increments. Both emerge from repeated independent randomness:

  • Bernoulli \( \to \) Poisson process
  • Random walk with small steps \( \to \) Brownian motion

The parameter \( \lambda \) in the Poisson process controls the rate of events, while the diffusion constant in Brownian motion (here equal to 1) controls the spread of fluctuations.
