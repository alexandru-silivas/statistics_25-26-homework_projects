Homework 11

Simulating Brownian Motion via the Euler–Maruyama Method

In this homework, we use the general stochastic differential equation simulator developed earlier to simulate a Wiener process (standard Brownian motion). Brownian motion is the continuous-time limit of random walks, defined by the SDE:

$$ dX_t = dW_t, \qquad X_0 = 0 $$

The Euler–Maruyama method for a general SDE $$ dX_t = \mu(X_t,t)\,dt + \sigma(X_t,t)\,dW_t $$ becomes

$$ X_{t+\Delta t} = X_t + \mu(X_t,t)\Delta t + \sigma(X_t,t)\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z $$

For Brownian motion, we have \( \mu = 0 \) and \( \sigma = 1 \), giving:

$$ X_{t+\Delta t} = X_t + \sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z, \quad Z \sim N(0,1). $$

This makes the simulation extremely efficient. Each step adds a normally distributed jump of variance proportional to the time step. Below is an interactive graph that generates new Brownian paths whenever you press Simulate.
