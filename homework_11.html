Homework 11 — Simulating Brownian Motion

In this assignment we use the Euler–Maruyama method to simulate a **Wiener process** (Brownian motion), defined mathematically as a stochastic process \( W_t \) satisfying:

\( W_0 = 0,\quad W_t - W_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\, t-s),\quad \text{independent increments}. \)

To simulate Brownian motion numerically, we discretize the interval \([0,T]\) into \(n\) pieces and approximate:

\( W_{t_{k+1}} = W_{t_k} + \sqrt{\Delta t}\, Z_k,\qquad Z_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1). \)

This is precisely the Euler–Maruyama scheme applied to the stochastic differential equation:

\( dW_t = dB_t \)
